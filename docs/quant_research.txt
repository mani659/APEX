Mean Reversion Behavioral Research Timeline
Research Log & Experimental Progression
Research Objective
The objective of this research was to investigate whether statistically significant behavioral mean reversion patterns exist in liquid financial markets, and whether these patterns can survive:
multi-stage filtering
volatility regime shifts
execution degradation
spread friction
temporal robustness tests
cross-market transfer tests
The broader hypothesis evolved into:
“Extreme short-term panic displacement followed by immediate recoil persistence may represent a repeatable behavioral asymmetry in liquid markets.”
Phase 1 — Initial Signal Discovery
Initial Hypothesis
Research began with the assumption that:
deep z-score displacements
combined with panic momentum
during elevated volatility
may produce asymmetric mean reversion behavior.
The earliest tests focused on:
z-score depth
ATR volatility state
momentum exhaustion
session timing
