## 5. Scientific Interpretation
1. **Is the initial entry genuinely negative?**
Yes, the standalone entry has a win rate of 35.1% and a negative mean expectancy over a fixed 240-bar window.

2. **Does the frozen recovery architecture create the positive expectancy?**
Yes, the normal production architecture shifts the mean PnL from -0.00048 to 0.00057 and the win rate to 89.0%.

3. **Which recovery component contributes the most?**
The R-Multiple Payoff Architecture and Aging Exits. Strikingly, the grid expansion did not trigger a single time (Maximum inventory depth remained 1). The entire positive outcome was generated by the exit logic (Inventory Management) harvesting small favourable excursions (MFE) quickly while holding adverse excursions (MAE) until aging limits or deep stops were hit.

4. **How many initially losing trades are rescued?**
54.4% of the population were initial losers (in the 240-bar baseline) that were successfully exited for a profit by the inventory management layer before the 240 bars elapsed.

5. **What proportion of profitability depends on rescue rather than signal quality?**
100%. The initial entry alpha is negative. The profitability is an illusion created by taking profits quickly (high win rate) and holding losers.

6. **What is the tail-risk cost of obtaining that rescue?**
While the win rate is artificially high (89%), the tail-risk cost is severe. The worst basket resulted in a realized loss of -0.06983 (nearly 700 pips), wiping out hundreds of small wins. This demonstrates that the strategy possesses severe negative skew.

## Final Verdict
**Outcome A — Recovery Dominant:** Initial entry is negative, but the existing recovery architecture (specifically the asymmetrical exit logic, not grid expansion) creates the majority of positive expectancy.
